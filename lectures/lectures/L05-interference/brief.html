Lecture scope

  • Define SUTVA and violations of SUTVA.

  • Give a “table” view of potential outcomes with vs. without SUTVA

  • Motivate the study of interference by giving an overview of forms of interference: – (social) networks – markets – spatial interference – symbiosis bias

For each of the above examples, focus on intuition-level explanation, a citation to an example, then formalize into the language of potential outcomes.

  • Define exposure mappings as a modelling approach; define network potential outcomes (“network exposure to treatment”; “network exposure to control”). Note that this is a modelling approach and there are others. Brief forward pointer to the fact that low-order outcomes will be discussed next lecture.

  • As an alternative to exposure mapping, consider Karrer et al. (2020)’s definition of Cluster SUTVA. Note that it’s under-studied (or: find work/citations downstream of Karrer et al. that use/study “cluster SUTVA”).

Testing interference. - Sharp-null testing associated with Eckles et al. - Randomization over randomizations associated with Saveski et al. - Staggered-rollout interference testing associated with Li et al.

Cluster-randomized experiments. - Graph cluster randomization and randomized graph cluster randomization. - Causal clustering associated with Viviano et al.; note that it’s an optimization problem wrt a specific estimator. - Conflict graph designs associated with Harshaw et al.
