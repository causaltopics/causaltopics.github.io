Positivity, Trimming, Target Populations, and Partial Identification

2026-10-07

Lecture scope

  • For starters, observe the IPW-based estimators rely on positivity. Without positivity, what happens? With very weak positivity, meaning some inverse probabilities are extremely large, what happens?

  • Have a block explaining the difference between “positivity” and “overlap”.

  • Frame this whole lecture in terms of design-based causal inference under randomized experiments, with secondary nods to observational causal inference.

Clipping and trimming - Introduce weight clipping, with citations. Winsorizing. - Trimming estimands and estimators, in the style of Crump et al. (2009). - Define the population in equipoise, and why the ATE for that population is sometimes a more principled estimand than the ATE for a trimmed population.

Separate approach to positivity violations: boundedness or other assumptions. - Manski bounds - Lee bounds - Inference under partial identification, generally. (define “partial identification”) - Lipschitz outcomes in the style of Khan, Saveski, Ugander (2024). - Connect to Bias-bias-variance tradeoffs in the style of Arbour, Parikh, Niknam, Sutart, Rudolph, and Feller.
