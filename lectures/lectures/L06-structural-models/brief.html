Estimation with Structural Models

2026-10-28

Lecture scope

The main goal of this paper is to introduce the idea of low-order outcome models as introduced by Cortez-Rodriguez et al. This lecture is relatively speaking more of a “single paper” lecture.

The idea is to step through the assumption of potential outcomes having low-order SUTVA violations, as low-order polynomials of the treatment variables. This approach then turns into a “parameter estimation problem”, of sorts, given the coefficients that parameterize the low-order polynomials. And then rolling up the parameter estimates into a GATE estimand works well.

Explain the psuedoinverse estimator and how it turns everything into a least squares problem.

Give a slight pointer to how the work generalizes beyond unit randomization, per Eichhorn et al., and how the approach can be adapted to handle covariate adjustment, per Wang and Li (2026).

Also review citations into Cortez-Rodriguez et al. and note any other new work downstream of that original paper.

Finally, note that causal inference under interference can also benefit from other non-parametric/structural assumptions, with Monotonoe interference as an example. See the literature list for papers that use monotonity assumptions, and give a concise review of that work.
